# Craps economics, break-even activity and inactive emission

> For the pre-reserve craps-only accounting and the distinction between player returns and vault comp issuance, use the [craps emissions report](CRAPS-EMISSIONS-REPORT.md). The 500k price and 21× mean high exposure are retained. This earlier report also discusses downstream outcomes outside that scope.

Analysis date: 2026-09-28. Historical pre-reserve snapshot, including the fee-only jackpot high allocation and revised high comps. No production changes were made for this analysis.

## Executive summary

The system deliberately issues value at low participation. Its fixed daily policy is **50,000 FLIP for ordinary battles/progressive, plus jackpot Added**. Added is at least **150,000 at levels 0–1**, or **50,000 later**. Thus the fixed expected capital allocation is **200,000 / 100,000 FLIP per opened day**, before engine losses, automatic protocol seats, action recycling and external rewards.

Using an illustrative **18% loss on bankroll exposed to the engine**, the model estimates:

- With no outside play and one unfunded normal house seat: **about 210,700 / 119,500 FLIP of net new commitments per day**, early/later. These are expectation estimates, not maximum possible payouts.
- Normal future passes at 25,000 each: break-even around **210 / 119 passes played per day**, early/later, including that same house allowance. At a more conservative 16% engine loss, these rise to **297 / 168**.
- Contested high future passes at 500,000 each create roughly **5,100 FLIP of additional commitments per pass at 18% loss**, before Coinflip and before their share of fixed subsidies. High passes bought at the actual live entry price instead retire about **16,200** each. The retail discount matters materially.
- Coinflip changes the final result. At 18% engine loss, an ordinary non-bonus Coinflip can turn that high-retail marginal issuance into roughly **2,500–2,700 net retirement**. A +2 bonus Coinflip leaves roughly **2,200–2,300 issuance**. There is no turnout-only break-even independent of the mix of buyers and payout days.
- The new high comps send **80% of a 12% loss budget** to comps. At an actual 18% engine loss this is **53.3% of the actual expected high-extra loss**, not 80%.

Priority decisions: distinguish a pre-Coinflip funding target from eventual token issuance; decide whether the high retail discount should rely on downstream Coinflip losses; and choose whether comp policy means most of a conservative budget or most of measured loss.

## Accounting boundary and assumptions

The primary tables measure **FLIP-denominated value committed, before the next Coinflip wager**. Positive net means new commitments; negative means value retired. Craps burns existing FLIP/backing or consumes a pass/comp entitlement, and pays Coinflip stakes and sometimes new passes. It does not mint liquid ERC20 FLIP directly.

Progressive funding is counted when allocated. Its eventual payment releases that liability and is not counted again. Comp allowance is carried at face value when credited; spending it consumes that allowance and substitutes a seat, pass or donation. Pass-funded entry similarly consumes an existing claim; the pass award and all its constituent entry fees must not both be recorded as new funding. This is an accounting model, not a forecast of circulating supply or the market value of a pass.

Baseline assumptions:

- A complete day has five ordinary windows and one jackpot; daily opening and settlement continue.
- Activity has been steady long enough to fill the trailing seven-day book. Abrupt changes are discussed below.
- Engine loss `e` applies to exposed bankroll after the natural and rotating shooter boosts, before purchase boons. It is held equal across simulated funding allocations for sensitivity analysis.
- All protocol awards are admitted at full standing. Standing-denied amounts generally move into, or remain in, the progressive, rather than disappearing.
- Jackpot multiplier outcomes, 300-FLIP bankroll rounding and engine bankroll caps are calculated explicitly. Ordinary preset means are exact; ordinary boost rounding to 100/1,000 FLIP is omitted.
- Tables explicitly include or exclude an unfunded normal house seat. A permanently unfunded seat is a conservative funding assumption, not a prediction of the automatic house balance trajectory. Its wins or saved passes can pay for later entries.
- Boons, quest rewards, record funding and passes supplied by other products are separately itemized. The main table is not advertised as an exact all-products supply forecast.

## Engine EV evidence

A fresh **12,000,000-run economic simulation** sampled two board strategies at field sizes 1, 40 and 200, two million runs per cell. It reused only the engine portion of the existing C++ replica, with the current **1,000-roll between-shooter budget**, 512-shooter cap, 32-bit escalator, current natural boost table and 5% rotation uplift. It did not run the legacy simulator's obsolete seven-window funding/pricing model.

| Board | Seats in field | Sampled bankroll loss | Standard error across 20 blocks, percentage points |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All chips random | 1 | 17.52% | 0.79 |
| All chips random | 40 | 17.86% | 0.80 |
| All chips random | 200 | 18.25% | 0.80 |
| 3 Place 4, 1 Place 5, 3 Place 10; remainder random | 1 | 18.16% | 0.35 |
| Same selected board | 40 | 18.52% | 0.36 |
| Same selected board | 200 | 18.87% | 0.35 |

These are estimates, not certified loss floors. The replica replaces keccak with a fast random mixer. It is not a fresh EVM parity proof or a search of every legal board. Returns have heavy tails: even a two-million-run mean can move substantially when a rare large win lands. Block errors do not prove that the tail is fully sampled. **18% is a useful illustrative case, 16% a lower-loss sensitivity, and 14% a stress case.** Board composition, field size and jackpot capital distribution can change the effective rate.

## Mechanism analysis: where the money goes

### Normal day

The exact ordinary preset averages are:

| Component | FLIP per normal full day |
|---|---:|
| Five ordinary bankrolls | 10,860 |
| Five ordinary bounties | 5,965 |
| Jackpot base entry | 8,000 |
| Total nominal entry value | **24,825** |
| Future retail price | **25,000** |

The ordinary bankroll loss at `e = 18%` is 1,954.8 FLIP. Bounties are redistributed, not lost to the engine, except extra bounties in a sole-high lane. Before subsidies, boons and downstream Coinflip, ordinary bankroll plus bounty return is about **88.38%** of the ordinary entry price for an exchangeable cohort. An individual's pot EV depends on their probability of winning, including board and standing differences; it is not automatically an equal share.

Jackpot base allocation for `P` paid seats and `F` awards is:

```
main capital = M × (8,000P + Added)
base bankroll = floor(main capital / (P + F) / 600) × 300
```

The engine cap can reduce that bankroll further; excess goes to the main pot. Every high roller counts once in `P`. The lottery has mean `E[M] = 1`: 90% at 0.5, 9% at 3, 0.9% at 20, 0.1% at 100. The draw is fair in EV, despite usually removing half the funded capital. It is incorrect to book the usual 0.5 outcome as a permanent 50% house edge.

Ignoring small bankroll-granule effects, the jackpot pays back `(1 − e/2)` of its capital. At 18% engine loss, that is **91%** before its progressive/record eligibility. A symmetric base seat's local jackpot value is approximately `0.91 × (8,000P + Added)/(P + F)`. This is a cohort benchmark, not an individual guaranteed probability of winning.

At large paid fields, an unsubsidized normal full day's engine/pot return is approximately **22,150 FLIP** at 18% loss: 89.23% of live nominal fees, or 88.60% of the 25,000 future price. Main boosts, jackpot Added, progressive claims, boons and quests are additional sources of player EV.

### Ordinary subsidies and comps

The ordinary main subsidy starts at **50,000 each opened day**. Action adds 12% of the prior seven-day daily average. Ordinary high action's 12% is routed 40% to the main allocation and 60% to high boosts. Half of the main allocation goes to the ordinary-window ladder and half to the progressive. The jackpot has separate Added and receives no second ordinary boost allocation.

Ordinary ladder odds are 76.8% at 0.25×, 20.8% at 1×, 2% at 10× and 0.4% at 100×: also mean one. These are different from the jackpot-capital lottery.

Ordinary eligible bankroll creates another **2% comp allowance**. Hence ordinary bankroll generally gives back **14 percentage points** through future boosts/progressive and comps. At an 18% engine loss only about **4% of ordinary bankroll remains** to offset fixed subsidies, before boons and downstream Coinflip. A sole ordinary high's extra bounty is action for the 12% book but not eligible bankroll for the 2% comp calculation. Its protocol high boost also rides the engine; the model includes that reduced expected payout.

Jackpot base fees are booked conservatively: Added and multiplier gains are excluded, and the 0.5 roll reduces booked fee action. Consequently `E[min(M,1)] = 0.55`. Ignoring granules, one 8,000 base fee produces **4,000 engine-risk capital but only 2,200 booked action**, not 4,000 booked action. That distinction materially affects break-even.

### Extra jackpot high exposure

Extra fee value per high seat is `X = (H − 1) × 8,000`, where `H` is 10 with probability 79/90 or 100 with probability 11/90; `E[H] = 21`.

| Case | Engine-risk capital in EV | High pot treatment | Comp allowance | Retained expected loss at e = 18% |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| At least two high rollers | `X/2` | Other half redistributes | `4.8% × X` | `4.2% × X` |
| One high roller | `X` | Bounty also rides run | `9.6% × X` | `8.4% × X` |

The extra high units receive no Added, no jackpot high boost and no multiplied purchase boon, and do not feed the future action book. Each high roller retains one ordinary base seat. A large high multiple therefore cannot multiply their share of Added, though many distinct base seats can still compete for it.

The extra contested wager's player return is approximately **91%** at an 18% engine loss; the sole-high extra return is approximately **82%**. Those percentages are before Coinflip and exclude the player's ordinary base seat and any later comp grant. Comps belong to a shared allowance administered through the vault, not automatically to the losing player.

Comp credit is `9.6%` of exposed high-extra capital. Thus it represents `9.6/e` of actual expected engine loss: **60% at e=16%, 53.3% at e=18%, and 48% at e=20%**. The implementation guarantees 80% of its conservative budget, not 80% of the realized or true expected loss. If “most loss” means more than half for every possible strategy mix, that is not established by the present calibration.

### Other economic channels

- **Purchase boons:** 5%, 10% or 15% of a paid run's bankroll return, with the return basis capped at 60,000 per window; maximum bonus 9,000 per window, 54,000 over six windows. Busts pay none. Ordinary high returns may reach the cap more often; jackpot high extras are excluded. These are additional issuance, not part of the sampled engine rate. At 18% loss, an uncapped 15% boon on every ordinary bank return could add `0.15 × 0.82 × 10,860 = 1,335.78` per normal day before the jackpot boon—larger than the modeled no-boon per-day operating margin. Frequency matters, so it cannot be assumed free.
- **Quests:** a qualifying offered daily join quest pays 100 FLIP; a qualifying offered level day-pass quest pays 800 once for that level, and a paired already-complete quest can also release. Full-day purchases additionally advance streak (+1 normal/+5 high). These rewards and their frequency belong in the user's EV and in a global issuance ledger; they are not a universal percentage of handle.
- **Progressive:** five ordinary windows and the jackpot can pay 5% of the live pool at 25× peak or 10% at 120×, subject to standing and pass conversion. A payout releases previously counted funding.
- **Dice-run record:** draws from Coinflip's existing shared record pool at new qualifying 100× records. That pool starts at 10,000 and receives 2,000 per resolved Coinflip day, shared with other record categories. Attribute that funding once globally; allocating the entire drip to craps adds at most 2,000/day to this report's funding burden, not an independent fresh pool for each winner. There is also a separate sDGNRS-denominated record benefit, outside the FLIP model.
- **Custom battles:** posted bankrolls/bounties/donations fund play; no scheduled 50,000 subsidy, 12% action recycling, jackpot Added or scheduled progressive/record access. Eligible bankroll still creates 2% comps, and paid entries may consume boons. Engine loss depends on selected depth/goal and the absence of scheduled shooter boosts; the scheduled 18% estimate is not a custom-table calibration. Net local retirement is engine loss less comp/boon/quest costs, with contested bounties and funded donations cancelling as transfers.
- **Lootbox/level/pass grants:** existing funded entitlements can pay for craps. Credit the grant's cost once in the originating product or as a transfer into this budget. Treating gifted seats as new cash sales gives a false break-even.

## Break-even activity

Ignoring jackpot bankroll granules, one normal day provides:

```
engine-risk bankroll ≈ 10,860 + 4,000 = 14,860
action/comp basis ≈ 10,860 + 2,200 = 13,060
recycled value ≈ 14% × 13,060 = 1,828.4
retained contribution at live entry fees ≈ e × 14,860 − 1,828.4
retained contribution at 25k future price ≈ above + 175
fixed cost ≈ 50,000 + (1 − e/2) × Added
```

An unfunded normal house entry adds roughly `24,825 − retained live contribution` to the daily cost. The computed tables below use the exact four jackpot rolls, bankroll granules and cap rather than that approximation.

**Normal future passes, 25,000 each; one unfunded normal house seat; before Coinflip:**

| Engine bankroll loss | Added 50,000: passes/day | Paid volume/day | Added 150,000: passes/day | Paid volume/day |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 14% stress | 285 | 7.125m | 504 | 12.600m |
| 16% lower-loss case | 168 | 4.200m | 297 | 7.425m |
| **18% illustrative case** | **119** | **2.975m** | **210** | **5.250m** |
| 20% higher-loss case | 92 | 2.300m | 161 | 4.025m |

At 18%, paying actual live prices rather than the 25,000 future retail price requires approximately **144 / 253 full-day buyers**, later/early. Without the unfunded-house allowance, the future-pass counts fall to **95 / 186**. These are days of participation consumed daily, not necessarily unique new people or passes purchased that calendar day.

At 18%, with an unfunded house and 150,000 Added, 100 normal future days leave about **110,200 issuance/day**, 200 leave **9,200 issuance/day**, and 300 produce about **92,200 retirement/day**. With 50,000 Added, those become approximately **18,600 issuance**, **82,800 retirement**, and **184,200 retirement**.

### High retail is a different slope

The high future retail price is 500,000, versus **521,325 expected live entry value**. That is a **21,325 discount per high day**. For a contested high field, approximate at-risk capital is `21 × 10,860 + 4,000 + 80,000 = 312,060`; recycled commitments are about 39,916.4. Therefore:

```
incremental high-future issuance ≈ 61,241.4 − e × 312,060
```

This turns negative only above approximately **19.62% engine loss**, before Coinflip, boons and fixed subsidies. At 18%, high live fees retire about 16,200 per additional day; the future discount turns that into about **5,100 issuance**. At 16% the future pass adds about **11,300 issuance**. These are aggregate margins including comp and future-boost commitments, not a promise that the individual high buyer earns a profit.

A single high roller is a different product economically: both extra bounties ride its run, and its ordinary high boost is also at risk. The model includes this distinction. It must not be used to claim the contested high-retail field has the same return.

For mixed participation at 18%, a useful pre-Coinflip estimate is:

```
net issuance ≈ fixed inactive cost
             − about 1,000 × normal future days
             + about 5,100 × contested high future days
```

Roughly **five additional normal future day buyers** offset one additional discounted high future day before covering the fixed subsidy. This slope is conditional on the board/field mix, full-standing awards, steady action and no boons/quests.

### Downstream Coinflip sensitivity

Craps liquid credits become a stake in a later Coinflip. From the current payout distribution, one unbonused flip has expected return **98.425%**; the +2 and +6 reward-percentage days give **99.425%** and **101.425%**. These are one-flip figures, not indefinite auto-rebuy yields. Craps credit does not itself qualify as a new manual deposit for a Coinflip boon or record.

The following sensitivity marks engine/pot credits through one flip, and marks between half and all of boost/progressive value through that flip; the rest remains pass face value. Comps remain at face value. This bounds the unknown protocol-award pass/liquid mix for the assumed payout-day type; it is not a fully resolved lifetime pass valuation.

| At 18% engine loss | Added 50k: normal future days to break even | Added 150k: normal future days to break even | Marginal contested high future day |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before Coinflip | 119 | 210 | +5,100 issuance |
| All relevant flips ordinary | 86–87 | 151–152 | 2,500–2,700 retirement |
| All relevant flips +2 | 104–105 | 184–185 | 2,200–2,300 issuance |

At 16% engine loss, even the ordinary Coinflip case still leaves roughly **3,500–3,700 issuance per discounted high future day**. A +6 day increases marginal high issuance at 18% to about **11,900–12,100**. Actual payout timing, progressive claim timing and automatic recycling require a stateful simulation before calling any of these unconditional steady-state token-supply forecasts.

## No outside activity and maximum emission

“No outside activity” here means no new outside craps purchases, with daily processing still running. It does not erase existing pass balances, comp allowance, progressive balances or prior action. No transactions means no automatic on-chain issuance by passage of time alone. No outside game purchases also eventually triggers the game's existing inactivity/terminal lifecycle; these daily rates are not an assertion that an abandoned game opens new days forever.

### Daily planning rate, not a hard cap

| Item | Levels 0–1, floor binding | Later levels, floor binding |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary fixed subsidy, including progressive | 50,000 | 50,000 |
| Jackpot Added, before its multiplier | 150,000 | 50,000 |
| **Fixed expected capital allocated** | **200,000** | **100,000** |
| **Modeled net commitments at 18% engine loss, plus one unfunded house** | **210,681/day** | **119,503/day** |
| Same at 16% engine loss | 212,455/day | 120,297/day |

The latter rows include house-generated action recycling and comp allowance, count the progressive when funded, and exclude the shared record drip. They assume eligible award queues; with none, the 18% estimates are approximately **210,510 / 119,493** because the house's paid-field seat can still receive the jackpot allocation. Award-count differences mainly change bankroll-rounding here. If the house pays from backing, subtract that funding instead of treating its entry as free.

The house's fallback is **one unfunded normal seat** when neither saved passes nor FLIP/backing can cover it; the vault sits out if it cannot pay. The seat creates bankroll exposure and bounty/fee value despite the absent burn. The expected quoted day is 24,825, and its largest live normal schedule is 48,000 (five 8,000 ordinary entries plus the jackpot fee). Its day returns and subsequent Coinflip/backing use determine how often fallback is needed.

### Startup and accumulated balances

- FLIP starts with **4,960,000 of comp allowance**: 200 normal reward-pass values.
- Craps starts with **20 normal passes each for house and vault**: 40 × 24,800 = **992,000 face value**, with 993,000 expected nominal entry value.
- Thus there are about **5,952,000 of initial craps comp/pass commitments** besides daily funding. Spending them is a release/use of that initial budget, not another 5.952m of new daily budget. The vault can deploy comp allowance without outside paid play, and high future reservations can buy discounted entry EV.
- Coinflip's separate genesis stakes—200,000 per body per day for 20 days, **8m total**—and its shared record pool are broader protocol seeds. They affect body funding and global supply, but are not newly created by craps.
- A day following a busy week can still receive large trailing-action boosts with zero play today. Existing high passes, progressive funds and comp grants also survive the activity drop. A cold zero-history day and a suddenly inactive mature day have different costs.

### There is no flat 200,000-FLIP daily payout ceiling

At zero trailing action, the ordinary ladder's 25,000 share can pay roughly **2.5m** if all five rungs hit 100×, while another 25,000 is booked to the progressive. Jackpot Added can become **15m early / 5m later** on its 100× capital draw. That is roughly **17.5m / 7.5m of rolled protocol capital**, plus progressive funding, before the automatic-seat capital and the dice-run outcomes. Ordinary granule rounding slightly changes those numbers.

Those figures are **not maximum emissions**. A run can pay above its allocated bankroll and above its 5× goal; the goal protects a reserve rather than immediately stopping play. Existing progressive/record balances and comp/pass stock can also be released. Runs have finite roll/shooter and integer bounds, but there is no small fixed per-day monetary cap enforced across all of these channels. An exact attainable worst-day payout requires the starting state, permitted calls, eligible queues and the engine's full payout bounds. Reporting 200k, 15m or 17.5m as that cap would be incorrect.

## Actor analysis

| Actor | Incentive and likely behavior | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Variance-seeking player | Buys runs and enjoys jackpot/boost tails | Funds bankroll loss; shared dice make cash flow volatile |
| EV maximizer | Chooses low-attendance fields, boards and eligible boons/quests | Captures subsidy; may reduce the effective edge relative to an average-player model |
| Whale/high buyer | Values full high day discount, high pot and future comps | Supplies fee-funded high risk, but discount can exceed retained loss before Coinflip |
| Affiliate/promoter | Brings users from broader game activity | Helps if users fund participation; no separate direct craps referral payout was identified in the examined entry path |
| Griefer | Pays to crowd fields, alter competition or impose settlement work | Costs real entry value, but can change timing and correlations; not a free increase in Added |
| Competitor/coordinated group | Uses many established wallets or sparse eligible queues | Can redirect main prizes/free seats; high multiple alone no longer amplifies Added exposure |
| Late entrant | Enters a known-price low-turnout window before RNG commitment | Can improve conditional subsidy EV; cannot know the settling dice |
| Vault/house | Recycles winnings, passes and shared comps | Keeps fields alive; free house fallback is a recurring subsidy, not external revenue |

## Equilibrium and scenario analysis

**Growth:** normal cash-funded activity tends toward break-even if its retained margin stays positive. Added may itself rise with the recorded main-game pool, so the subsidy target is not fixed at its floor. More high retail activity is not interchangeable with more normal activity.

**Low attendance:** large per-seat subsidy attracts EV seekers, which dilutes that subsidy. Shared dice and unequal standing/boards mean equal-wallet pot splits are only a benchmark. The house and awarded seats receive a meaningful portion of low-turnout value.

**High-heavy coordination:** extra high principal is isolated from Added, so the prior amplification is removed. Multiple high seats restore the bounty half to pure redistribution, which is materially more favorable than a sole high riding the whole extra fee. The 500k price and comp policy must therefore be evaluated on contested fields.

**Decline:** the seven-day book responds with a lag. For constant old action followed by zero new booked action, the old-action contribution decays roughly through 7/7, 6/7, …, 1/7, then zero. Protocol seats or comp-funded play keep it above literal zero. Progressive and comp balances can continue paying after new fee flow falls.

**Maturity:** the progressive is an inventory of already-allocated value. Large winning payouts make realized daily issuance bursty even if the long-run accounting is balanced. There is no reason to equate a day's cash payout with its new liability creation.

**Feedback risk:** newly granted comps/passes can generate more booked action, so an accounting system that calls every redeemed entitlement new cash revenue can hide continued issuance. This is bounded per entry by the grant budgets/rates; it is not evidence of runaway growth by itself. The new jackpot high extra avoids one double-spend by directing its loss budget to comps without also funding future boosts.

## Risk matrix and recommendations

| Priority / likelihood | Issue | Economic impact | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| High / present under modeled low-loss and bonus-day mixes | High future price below retained-value-neutral price | More high retail play can increase net issuance | Decide target accounting basis; reevaluate retail discount jointly with comps and Coinflip day mix |
| High / certain policy behavior | No flat daily payout ceiling | Large tails despite modest average budget | Publish expected allocation separately from state-dependent payout exposure; stress existing balances and correlated dice |
| Medium / present | Old 80-ticket break-even material is stale | Underestimates current needed activity | Replace it with current six-window, Added, comp, price and house assumptions |
| Medium / plausible | Boon/quest users cluster in favorable fields | Erodes margin relative to no-boon scenarios | Measure boon-bearing bankroll payout and incremental quest issuance per paid day |
| Medium / observed sampling limitation | Engine mean and rare tail uncertainty | Small rate errors materially move break-even | Broaden board search and current-EVM-verified multi-seed calibration before fixing production policy to one rate |
| Medium / present | “80% to comps” differs from 80% of actual loss | Misstates recycling policy | Monitor loss by exposed capital; specify conservative-budget share versus measured-loss share explicitly |

At 18% loss a contested high retail price around **505,100** merely covers its own marginal pre-Coinflip commitments; at 16% this is about **511,300**. These exclude a contribution to fixed Added/base subsidy, boons and quests. Removing the current discount by pricing near 521,325 would restore a positive marginal contribution in these scenarios, but that is a product decision, not a change made by this analysis.

Monitor actual burns/backing consumption separately from redeemed pass/comp value; bankroll at risk separately from contested bounties; Added and ordinary subsidy separately; expected versus realized engine returns; comp grants and spending; progressive funded/paid/retained; high retail versus live revenue; boon/quest costs; and each credit's downstream Coinflip day type. A 30/90-day net value ledger should reconcile opening liabilities + new allocations + actual funding − payouts − closing liabilities, with transfers between products explicitly named.

## Reproduction and limitations

Run:

```sh
python3 scripts/craps-engine-ev-calibration.py
python3 scripts/craps-ev-analysis.py --plot
```

Artifacts: [model inputs and results](CRAPS-EV-2026-09-28.json), [engine samples](CRAPS-ENGINE-EV-2026-09-28.tsv), [chart](CRAPS-EV-2026-09-28.png), [vector chart](CRAPS-EV-2026-09-28.svg).

The funding model is deterministic conditional on its loss rate. It checks multiplier means and preset arithmetic, enumerates jackpot rolls/granules/caps, and distinguishes sole/contested high risk and the two purchase prices. It is not a fully stateful simulation of comp spending, pass redemption, body balances, Coinflip auto-rebuy, dynamic Added, or arbitrary board/boon selection. It does not claim a proven absolute maximum payout.

The existing `scripts/craps-high-water-system-sim.cpp` system driver and `CrapsSystemEcon.test_F2_theBaseSubsidySetsABreakEvenTicketCount` still contain obsolete seven-window/pricing/15,600-action assumptions. Historical TSVs also predate later engine/funding changes. Their old system break-even outputs were not used. The newly added wrapper invokes only engine sampling and updates the roll bound from current Solidity. No existing production or historical simulation files were rewritten for this report.
